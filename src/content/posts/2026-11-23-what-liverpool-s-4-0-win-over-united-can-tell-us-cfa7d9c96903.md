By the 13th minute at Anfield, Liverpool were two goals up and Manchester United were down to ten men. Leny Yoro’s dismissal gave Dominik Szoboszlai the free kick from which he opened the scoring. Florian Wirtz added the second, and Liverpool went on to win 4–0. For Liverpool, the part worth taking into the next match was how quickly they turned an opening into a lead that changed the game.

**Analysis.** A red card gives the other team an extra player. A goal gives it the freedom to decide how much risk to take. Liverpool got both advantages in quick succession. Once Wirtz scored, United had to find a way back into the match while also covering for the missing player. That made every decision to commit someone forward more expensive.

**Analysis.** Liverpool still had work to do. Rio Ngumoha scored in the second half and substitute Víctor Muñoz added the fourth. A team ahead by two can spend the rest of a match protecting the lead. Liverpool scored twice more, and having two different players finish those chances is a detail worth keeping in view when the scoreline makes the afternoon look inevitable.

**Counterargument.** For Mauricio Pochettino, the result makes for a harsh first Premier League match in charge of United. The footage after the dismissal can help him ask how his team should defend with ten. He’ll need more evidence to judge how his usual shape stands up with eleven. Liverpool face a similar question going the other way. Their next opponent may leave fewer openings, and Liverpool may have to create them for longer before anyone scores.

**Analysis.** The useful lesson for Liverpool is how promptly they took the chances they were given. The next test is whether they can show the same precision when the match stays level and both sides have eleven players.
